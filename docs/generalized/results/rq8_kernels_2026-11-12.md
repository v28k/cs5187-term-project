DataFusion (`src/configs/datafusion_kernels.yaml`, kernels mined from the grammar only): rule pairs 241 -> 276 and, per 1000 test cases, 14.6 -> 19.2 (A12 1.00 for both, p 0.10), with fewer test cases in the kernel arm; this is the clearest throughput-neutral gain of the three engines.

Reading. On both engines every kernel trial reached more rule pairs than every plain trial. On SQLite the kernel arm also ran about twice as many test cases (composed kernels are small and fast), so part of its gain is throughput; on DuckDB the two arms ran a similar number of test cases (13.1k vs 13.8k), and kernels still reached 26 % more rule pairs and fired three rules the plain grammar never fired. Kernels reduce the variety of plan shapes on DuckDB (553 vs 446). No new bug candidate came from the kernel arm in these short runs (one TLP false positive with QUALIFY, fixed in eb79c8a; the others are known bugs). Per 1000 test cases (throughput-neutral) the SQLite gain almost disappears (4.9 vs 5.3 pairs, A12 0.56): on SQLite, kernels help mainly because they are cheap to run. On DuckDB the gain remains (21.7 vs 25.9, A12 0.78). Next: 5 trials x 1 h per arm.
